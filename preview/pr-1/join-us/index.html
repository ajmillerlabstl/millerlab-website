2027 – 2028 Graduate Student Opportunity

Miller Lab · Saint Louis University · Danforth Plant Science Center (St. Louis, Missouri, USA)

The Miller Lab, functioning as a joint faculty partnership between Saint Louis University and the Danforth Plant Science Center, anticipates recruiting one PhD student to join the lab in Fall 2027 pending funding availability.

Our work focuses on perennial plant biology, diversity, and evolution. The overarching aim and scientific rationale is to advance fundamental understanding of the genetic and environmental determinants of phenotypic variation in long-lived plants (perennials), with the goal of developing perennial crops that benefit people and the planet. At the core of our work are multi-year, multi-site field experiments where in-field phenotyping and lab-based sample processing are paired with genetics and modeling to test hypotheses about how perennial plants partition resources over time and how perennial plant traits influence the soil ecosphere and gas fluxes. Current research themes center on four long-term impacts: 1) adaptation and resilience in clonally propagated, woody perennials; 2) dual functionality (yield, ecosystem services) in emerging perennial herbaceous cash crops and cover crops; 3) phenomic prediction pipelines that expedite and optimize perennial crop improvement; and 4) conservation and accessibility of perennial crop genetic resources. Work underway at the moment focuses on grapevines (Vitis spp.), silflower (Silphium integrifolium), intermediate wheatgrass (Thinopyrum intermedium; tradename Kernza®), and assorted legumes, among others.

The graduate student will carry out independent research within ongoing projects and potentially develop new ones, pending funding. The successful applicant will have:

  • A commitment to sustainable food security
  • Passion for perennial plant biology, diversity, and evolution
  • A strong background in one or more of the following subject areas: agriculture, agroecology, agronomy, botany, ecology, evolution, genetics, genomics, plant biology (anatomy, morphology, physiology, etc.), and/or soil science, among others
  • Motivation and leadership capacity to work independently to develop and lead a project to completion, including literature review and synthesis, hypothesis generation, experimental design, data collection, analysis, manuscript preparation and presentation
  • Experience conducting research in lab and/or field settings
  • Demonstrated strengths in oral and written communication
  • Ability to work well as a member of a diverse team and commitment to work together to achieve project goals
  • Curiosity, interest in learning (including self-teaching), and a growth mindset
  • Strong work ethic and positive attitude
  • Willingness to bridge two distinct institutions located ~20 minutes from each other

To apply, please 1) submit an application to the Saint Louis University Graduate Program; and 2) send a cover letter including a statement of interest, as well as a CV/resume, to Allison Miller (amiller@danforthcenter.org) and Judy Mitchell (jmitchell@danforthcenter.org). The application deadline is December 15, 2026.

How to Reach Out

If you are interested in joining the lab, please send an email to Allison Miller (amiller@danforthcenter.org or allison.j.miller@slu.edu) with:

  • Your current CV or résumé.
  • A brief summary (1–2 paragraphs) describing your research background, academic path, and scientific interests.
  • A short note on why you are drawn to the Miller Lab's research in perennial plant biology, root systems, or crop evolution.
  • For graduate applicants: mention whether you hold or plan to apply for external fellowships (e.g., NSF GRFP).
