Rachel Tavares is a plant geneticist and microbiologist. Her research at the Donald Danforth Plant Science Center focuses on the genetics of key agronomic traits in Silphium integrifolium to inform sustainable crop breeding. During her PhD at the University of Massachusetts Amherst, Rachel studied beneficial plant-microbe interactions and their contributions to competitiveness in weedy rice. Rachel is interested in beneficial microbes as an innovative biofertilizer for crops. Rachel used quantitative genomic approaches to discover mechanisms of the domestication trait, seed shattering, in rice and used histology to understand what is happening at a cellular level. This expands off work she did as a NSF REU student in 2017 at the Danforth center with Dr. Elizabeth Kellogg and as a technician in the following year working with both Dr. Kellogg and Dr. Dimitri Nusinow prior to starting her graduate education. Rachel also works as a Reviewing editor for Applications in Plant Sciences focusing on improving and increasing access to scientific literature.
